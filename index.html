Hermès · Pop Up Event

Le Monde d'Hermès Kiosk

A traveling activation across three markets that welcomes guests to explore the brand.

The Le Monde d'Hermès kiosk was a recurring activation built to invite people to explore the brand. From 2023–2025, markets included San Francisco, Austin, and Aspen, centered around the brand's bi-annual magazine.

I ran point on the program, from RFP through execution, and defined how the program takes shape in the US.

Hermès · Store Openings, 2020–2026

Artist Barricades

15+ store openings, each paired with a local artist who turned construction walls into original work.

Every new Hermès store starts behind a barricade. This program turns that construction wall into a work of art: a local artist commissioned to create something beautiful ahead of the opening.

I sourced the artists, briefed them on the project and Hermès codes, then supported their proposal through asset delivery and install.

Artists across the program include Clark Richert (Aventura & Short Hills), Ellen Rutt (Troy), Damin Lujan (Costa Mesa), Sophie Roach & Laura Lit (Austin), Mike Perry (706 Madison, NYC), Math Bass (Topanga Canyon), Dahlia Elsayed (Princeton), Magalie Guerin (Chicago), Vanna Black (Atlanta), Danielle Hacche (Scottsdale), Tess Davies (Nashville), Liz Flores (Plaza del Lago), Kate Dehler (Dallas), Edward Acosta (Manhasset), and Gary Mullen (Washington D.C.).

Hermès · Out-of-Home

Meatpacking Billboard

One mural unit above 14th Street, recast for every campaign flight that comes through the district.

The M-62 unit sits above the Meatpacking District — a single mural surface that gets a new creative treatment with each campaign: Holiday 2025, February and May 2026, and a tie-in wrap for Mystery at the Grooms'.

I managed the vendor relationship and creative rotation, from proof-of-performance documentation to keeping the unit in step with whatever the brand was running that season.

Hermès · Immersive Event & Film

Mystery in the Making

“Mystery at the Grooms'” — a citywide immersive event and the film that came out of it, from Shanghai to a pier on the East River.

Mystery at the Grooms' took the brand's equestrian world and turned it into a scavenger-hunt-style immersive event — first in Shanghai, then in New York at Pier 36, June 19–29, 2025. The city got the campaign too: a hand-painted Meatpacking takeover, a Pier 36 venue wrap, an East Broadway subway takeover, a New York Times cover wrap, and a coloring-page tie-in with The New Yorker.

I worked the run of show — rehearsals through the public sessions, press brunch, and VIP dinners — and helped produce the recap film, interviewing Diane Mahady (President & CEO, Hermès Americas) and Charlotte David (VP Communications) along the way.

About

The intersection of creativity and marketing

My interest has always sat at the intersection of creativity and marketing — what people respond to, what they care about, and how art can be the driving force behind that. It's the thread running through six years at Hermès, from media coordinator to media manager, always closest to the moments where a brand becomes something people want to stop and look at.

What people respond to, what they care about, and how art can be the driving force behind that.

Before Hermès, that same interest showed up in a Psychology and World Arts and Cultures degree at UCLA — one half asking why people respond the way they do, the other looking at how art has always been part of the answer.

It shows up off the clock, too. I've been teaching yoga at CorePower for over a year — a different kind of practice in reading a room and building something people want to be part of.
